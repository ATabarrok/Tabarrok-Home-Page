How the comparison was made

I put greatest weight on a coherent research agenda that engages the department’s substantive interests, then on evidence of research execution and an independent pipeline, and then on teaching relevance. Published and accepted work provides stronger evidence of completion than work in progress; an R&R is identified as such. A single-authored JMP helps assess independence, but coauthored work is not disqualified. No numeric composite score is used because the available evidence does not support that precision.

The interpretation of GMU fit follows the requested areas and the department’s descriptions of Austrian, experimental, and public-choice economics, its research centers, and its interdisciplinary graduate approach. Fit means the questions and methods a candidate studies; it does not mean inferred political beliefs or agreement with particular findings. Personal characteristics and contact details were not used in the assessment.

PRIORITY 1

Matthias Weigand

Harvard University · PhD expected 2027

Economic history; political economy; organization and institutions

Why GMU: The strongest combination of GMU-relevant questions, a substantial research pipeline, and demonstrated publication quality in this collection. His work asks how institutional arrangements change incentives, coordination, state power, and long-run development. The cartel project connects market power and financing to investment and entry; the broader portfolio examines constitutional constraints, bureaucratic incentives, and unintended consequences of political intervention.

Evidence: Job market paper: Coordination and Collusion in Industrial Development, with Luis Bosshart. The CV lists an Econometrica publication, Crisis and Absolutism accepted at the Quarterly Journal of Economics, and Identity and Institutional Change as reject-and-resubmit at the American Economic Review. The last is not an acceptance. Teaching includes economic history and quantitative economics.

What to establish next: Ask him to distinguish his contribution to the coauthored projects and explain the institutional mechanisms in a price-theory seminar. Establish interest in GMU; this review does not predict recruitment feasibility.

PRIORITY 2

Shakked Noy

MIT · PhD expected 2027

Political economy; behavioral economics; information and nonpecuniary incentives

Why GMU: An unusually good bridge between GMU's interest in broad economic reasoning and experimental evidence. The social-media work treats status and expressive motives as incentives affecting the supply of content, rather than examining only audience behavior. Work on information reliability and AI productivity adds breadth beyond a single political-economy application.

Evidence: Job market paper: The Supply Side of Social Media, with Aakaash Rao. The CV lists The Transmission of Reliable and Unreliable Information as forthcoming in QJE, Experimental Evidence on the Productivity Effects of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Science (2023), and a JEP article on German industrial relations. Teaching experience includes graduate behavioral economics.

What to establish next: Probe the connection between individual experiments and equilibrium platform outcomes, and his own contribution to coauthored work. Assess plans for teaching undergraduate price theory.

PRIORITY 3

Cassidy Shubatt

Harvard University · PhD expected 2027

Experimental economics; behavioral theory; experimental methods

Why GMU: My first choice for a search centered on strengthening ICES. The research uses theory to design experiments that can actually distinguish competing explanations of choice. That is a contribution to experimental economics as a method, with a complementary agenda on complexity and valuation. Graduate experimental and microeconomic-theory teaching experience is directly relevant.

Evidence: Job market paper: Designing Experiments to Distinguish Theories, with Baiyun Jing. Other papers study tradeoffs, comparison complexity, and valuation. Quantifying Lottery Choice Complexity, with Benjamin Enke, is listed as revise-and-resubmit at Econometrica, not accepted. The October CV and website differ in the number of historical experiments analyzed; no sample-size claim is used in the ranking.

What to establish next: Ask how the design method generalizes beyond risky choice, and how much of the research program would involve institutional or market experiments. Clarify independent contributions to the coauthored pipeline.

Availability evidence: Explicitly on the 2026–27 market

PRIORITY 4

Giacomo Manferdini

Bocconi University · PhD 2021–2027

Political economy; behavioral microeconomics; persuasion and narratives

Why GMU: A particularly close bridge between public-choice questions and behavioral mechanisms. The agenda studies how political actors use causal narratives and memory to influence voters, with a coherent connection between theory and political applications. This offers potential collaboration across political economy and experimental economics without requiring those fields to be treated as separate searches.

Evidence: Job market paper: Blameocracy: Causal Rhetoric in Politics, with Francesco Bilotta and Alberto Binetti. Coarse Memory and Plausible Narratives, with Bilotta, is listed as forthcoming in AEJ: Microeconomics. Other projects address persuasion through memory and congressional rhetoric. Teaching includes master's game theory and econometrics, and undergraduate microeconomics and macroeconomics.

What to establish next: Separate the empirical contribution of the job market paper from the theory agenda. Discuss a single-authored research direction and how the agenda changes predictions about political competition.

Availability evidence: Current Bocconi market CV

PRIORITY 5

Hong Wang

Harvard University postdoc; Boston University PhD · PhD August 2026; postdoc August 2026–April 2027

Political entrepreneurship; economic history; markets for ideas

Why GMU: One of the most distinctive substantive GMU fits. Her research asks how entrepreneurs assemble and transmit political ideas, how institutions create constituencies for their own survival, and how communication technologies affect political outcomes. A theory of cultural matching complements historical empirical work. The postdoc is included under the requested broad scope.

Evidence: Job market paper: What Killed the ERA? The Role of Entrepreneurs in the Marketplace of Ideas and Policy. The CV also lists work on AM-radio measurement, the John Birch Society, and cultural matching. These are working papers; the CV does not list an accepted economics journal article. Teaching includes introductory microeconomics and macroeconomics.

What to establish next: Read the identification argument carefully, especially the distinction between signal propagation, measurement, and an exogenous instrument. Ask how the political-entrepreneurship model extends beyond the ERA case.

Availability evidence: Included in the 2026–27 economic-history market roundup; current market CV

PRIORITY 6

Purdue University · PhD expected 2027

Experimental economics; beliefs; coordination; information

Why GMU: A strong ICES match with a coherent portfolio on motivated beliefs, responsibility, coordination, and bargaining. Existing research with Alex Tabarrok provides a concrete connection to the department, although the recommendation rests on the research rather than the affiliation. The portfolio also opens a route from experiments to institutional questions about accountability and advice.

Evidence: Job market paper: Biased Agents, Extreme Beliefs: Motivated Reasoning Under Competing Models. The CV lists Sunk-cost, Responsibility, and Belief as revised and resubmitted to Experimental Economics, and Norms in Conflict: Why AI Advisors Fail to Improve Human Coordination, with Cason, Tabarrok, and Zubrickas, as R&R there. Neither is counted as accepted. Teaching includes principles and graduate microeconomics/game-theory assistance.

What to establish next: Ask about mechanisms that survive alternative belief-elicitation methods and about an independent follow-up agenda. Clarify the division of work in the existing GMU collaboration.

PRIORITY 7

Gailius Praninskas

London School of Economics · PhD expected 2027

Public choice; economic history; property rights; political selection

Why GMU: The research portfolio closely matches GMU's questions about who enters politics, what politicians maximize, and how property-rights arrangements affect economic outcomes. The enclosure project explicitly considers contracting and hold-up problems, while the political projects distinguish financial incentives from policy motives. This is substantive institutional economics, not simply historical data applied to a generic question.

Evidence: Job market paper: How Enfranchisement Led to Representation, with Ellen Munroe. Other projects address political dynasties, Welsh enclosure, and incentives to become a politician. The main economics projects are drafts; a separate AI-evaluation conference publication should not be mistaken for a refereed economics journal acceptance. The CV reports teaching in political economy, development, and microeconomics, plus LSE teaching awards.

What to establish next: Ask for the enclosure draft and for a clear account of contributions to the shared data and coauthored papers. Test whether the political-entry framework produces predictions beyond the British setting.

PRIORITY 8

Michael C. Wang

Yale University · PhD expected 2027

Price theory; industrial organization; dynamic pricing and information

Why GMU: The clearest addition if price theory is meant as a substantive hiring priority. His single-authored JMP explains product-line and upgrade patterns through buyers' strategic responses to sellers' inability to commit to future prices. The broader agenda links pricing, information, and platform competition, providing a strong complement to the historians and experimentalists on this list.

Evidence: Job market paper: A Coasian Theory of Coarse Product Lines. Screening and Segmenting: A Consumer Surplus Perspective, with Dirk Bergemann and Tibor Heumann, is published in the American Economic Review (2026). Other projects study cloud-service pricing and platform recommendation strategies. Teaching assistance includes graduate and undergraduate/MBA game theory.

What to establish next: Ask him to present the economic intuition and testable implications of the Coasian result, and discuss teaching an undergraduate price-theory course. Formal applied theory is the demonstrated strength; Austrian economics is not claimed.

Availability evidence: Explicitly on the 2026–27 market

PRIORITY 9

Riccardo Di Cato

UC San Diego · PhD expected 2027

Institutions; economic history; development; diffusion of knowledge

Why GMU: An appealing connection between historical development and GMU's interest in how knowledge and institutions jointly sustain markets. The banking project studies the interaction between missionary exposure and market institutions, including the change under central planning and subsequent reforms. Related work on religion, communication, and beliefs gives the agenda room to grow beyond banking history.

Evidence: Job market paper: Seeds of Market-Based Capitalism: Missionaries, Banks, and China's Modernization, with Jiachen Li. The CV lists development, political economy, and economic history, and teaching assistance in introductory and intermediate microeconomics. No accepted economics journal publication is listed in the reviewed CV.

What to establish next: Probe the separation of missionary knowledge, selection into locations, and other institutional effects. Ask what findings would distinguish the knowledge mechanism from competing explanations and identify the independent research pipeline.

PRIORITY 10

Myles Ellis

Brown University · PhD candidate; economics program 2021–present

Public choice; special-interest groups; political economy; institutional design

Why GMU: A strong external match for GMU’s interest in how political institutions and incentives shape policy. His job market paper examines reliance on legislation supplied by special-interest groups. A second project studies the costs of applying uniform policies to heterogeneous communities, while work on franchising connects organizational arrangements to incentives and outcomes.

Evidence: Job market paper: Copycat Legislation: The Rising Influence of Special Interest Groups on American Policy. Other working papers include The Cost of Policy Misalignment in Heterogeneous Communities, with Mattias Polborn, and Private Equity and the Financialization of Franchising, with J. Monroe Gamble IV. No accepted journal publication is listed in the reviewed CV. Teaching assistance includes applied economic analysis, intermediate macroeconomics, crisis economics, and financial institutions.

What to establish next: Probe the identification of legislative-capacity effects when politicians move from state government to Congress. Discuss his independent pipeline, completion timing, and preparation to teach price theory and public choice.

Availability evidence: Listed on Brown’s current job-market roster; CV updated October 7, 2026
